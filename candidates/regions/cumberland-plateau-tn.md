# Cumberland Plateau (Eastern Tennessee, Eastern Kentucky)

**Priority: 2**

## Why this region

Mild four-season climate, sandstone-over-limestone geology that produces reliable contact springs, and most rural counties are unzoned. Slightly more expensive than the Ozarks but warmer winters and longer growing season for organic crop production.

## Geology

Pennsylvanian sandstone caps Mississippian limestone. Springs emerge at the contact, often on the slopes below the plateau rim. The plateau itself is relatively flat (good for crops); the springs are typically along the drainage edges. Annual rainfall 50–55 inches.

Budget of $4M generally buys 1,000–1,800 acres on the Cumberland Plateau in TN.

## Zoning posture

Tennessee counties on the plateau are mostly unzoned outside municipal limits, but several have adopted county-wide zoning in the last decade — verify per parcel:

- Likely unzoned (still verify): Pickett, Fentress, Scott, Morgan, Van Buren
- Has some county zoning: Cumberland (parts), Bledsoe, Sequatchie

Eastern Kentucky counties are generally unzoned.

## Mineral rights climate

**This is the biggest watch-out for the region.** The Cumberland Plateau and eastern KY have a long history of coal, oil, and natural gas extraction. Severed mineral estates are extremely common — the surface owner often does not own the minerals, and surface-rights doctrine in TN and KY can let mineral owners disturb the surface to extract.

For any candidate here:
1. Mineral title opinion is mandatory and non-negotiable.
2. Check for active or expired oil/gas leases in the county recorder's index.
3. Check for adjacent strip-mining or longwall coal mining — subsidence and water-table effects extend across property lines.

Many otherwise-perfect parcels in this region will fail G5. Budget time accordingly.

## Spring inventory

- Tennessee Department of Environment and Conservation maintains a karst features and springs database.
- Kentucky Geological Survey publishes a karst atlas.

Springs on the plateau tend to emerge along the rim slopes — make sure the spring originates on the parcel and is not just collected from upstream.

## Climate (advantage over Ozarks)

USDA Zone 6b–7a. Longer growing season (~180–200 days frost-free) than the Ozarks (~170–190). Less ice, less extreme summer heat than southern Missouri. Better fit for diversified organic crops if climate is a tiebreaker.

## Watch-outs specific to this region

- **Severed minerals** (above) — single biggest disqualifier.
- **TVA easements** — Tennessee Valley Authority transmission lines crisscross the region. Check the EIA HIFLD layer for any line on the parcel; G7 will fail.
- **State park / state forest adjacency** — sometimes brings federal scenic easements. Check title carefully.
- **Coal-related water contamination** — historical mining can leave acid mine drainage in springs. If the parcel or its recharge area was ever surface-mined, water quality will be a problem regardless of flow.
