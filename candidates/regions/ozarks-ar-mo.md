# Ozark Plateau (Northern Arkansas, Southern Missouri)

**Priority: 1 (strongest fit)**

## Why this region first

The Ozarks combine the three things that make this search feasible: large karst springs, low land prices per acre, and a tradition of weak county-level land-use regulation. More 1,000-acre tracts at < $4,000/acre with named springs come up here than anywhere else in the country.

## Geology

Mississippian and Ordovician limestone and dolomite, dissected by the White, Buffalo, Current, and Eleven Point river systems. The plateau is mature karst — caves, sinkholes, sinking streams, and many first- and second-magnitude springs. Recharge is reliable thanks to 45–50 inches of annual rainfall.

## Typical land cost

| Sub-region                    | $/acre (raw, 1,000+ ac) | Notes                                                  |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| North-central AR (Searcy, Stone, Newton, Marion, Baxter cos.) | $1,500 – $3,500   | Best blend of springs and price                        |
| Southern MO (Ozark, Douglas, Wright, Texas, Shannon, Oregon, Carter cos.) | $1,200 – $3,000   | Often the lowest $/acre with springs                   |
| Northwest AR (Madison, Carroll, Washington cos.)              | $3,000 – $6,000+  | More expensive due to NW Arkansas growth corridor      |

Budget of $4M comfortably buys 1,000–2,500 acres in north-central AR or southern MO.

## Zoning posture

Most rural Ozark counties are **unzoned at the county level**. Land use is governed only by state law and any recorded covenants. Confirm county by county — exceptions exist near small cities (Mountain Home, West Plains) and within a few municipal extraterritorial jurisdictions.

Counties typically unzoned (verify with a planning-department letter for any specific parcel):
- Arkansas: Searcy, Stone, Newton, Madison (mostly), Marion, Baxter (outside city ETJ), Fulton, Sharp, Izard
- Missouri: Ozark, Douglas, Wright, Texas, Shannon, Oregon, Carter, Reynolds, Howell

## Mineral rights climate

Generally favorable. The Ozarks are not a major oil/gas producing region, and mineral severance is less common than in Appalachia. Lead/zinc mining occurred historically in southwest MO (Tri-State district) — avoid Jasper, Newton (MO), and Lawrence counties for that reason. Always order a mineral title opinion regardless.

## Spring inventory

- Arkansas Geological Survey publishes *Springs of Arkansas*; named springs are catalogued by county.
- Missouri DNR publishes a spring inventory; the largest are along the Current, Eleven Point, and Jacks Fork rivers.

Even small 1,000-acre tracts in this region commonly have one to three named springs and several smaller seeps. The standard for G9 is whether the named spring **originates on the parcel** and runs through the dry season — both must be confirmed in person.

## Watch-outs specific to this region

- **River-frontage parcels often have utility easements** along the river corridor for power or water — check carefully against G4 and G7.
- **Caves and sinkholes** can complicate building siting; not a disqualifier but plan around them.
- **Buffalo National River corridor** — federal scenic easements exist on parcels near the river. These fail G4. Avoid parcels in the BNR viewshed unless you confirm no easement.
- **Logging history** — most Ozark timber tracts have been cut at least once. Soil contamination from this is unlikely, but check for old log-loading areas if pursuing organic certification.
