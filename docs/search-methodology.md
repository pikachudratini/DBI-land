# Search Methodology

How to find candidate parcels efficiently and avoid wasting due-diligence dollars on properties that will never qualify.

## Channels (in priority order)

### Listing aggregators
The bulk of large rural listings will surface here. Set up saved searches with min acreage 1,000 and max price $4M.

- **LandWatch** (`landwatch.com`) — broadest coverage; supports min acreage and amenity filters (springs, creek, etc.).
- **Lands of America** (`landsofamerica.com`) — same parent company as LandWatch but separate listings.
- **Land.com** — same network.
- **LandSearch** (`landsearch.com`) — newer aggregator, sometimes catches listings the others miss.

### Specialty rural-land brokerages
These firms list large recreational and ranch properties; many never hit the public aggregators.

- **Hall and Hall** (`hallhall.com`) — high-end ranches, often above budget but worth scanning the South Central region.
- **Whitetail Properties** — strong in MO, AR, KY, TN, IL.
- **Mossy Oak Properties** — broad rural network, county-level franchises throughout the Ozarks and Appalachians.
- **AFM Real Estate** (American Forest Management) — timberland divestitures, often 1,000+ acre tracts.
- **Cabin Realty / Crye-Leike rural divisions** — Ozarks specialists.
- **Republic Ranches** — Texas-focused; included for completeness, but Hill Country usually exceeds budget.

### Off-market sources
Worth pursuing once Phase 1 of the listed market is exhausted.

- **County tax-delinquent rolls** — Counties publish lists of properties going to tax sale. Large rural parcels occasionally appear; mineral and easement risk is higher and must be diligenced especially carefully.
- **Timber-company divestitures** — Weyerhaeuser, Rayonier, Hancock Timber, and PotlatchDeltic periodically auction tracts. Sign up for their land-sale email lists.
- **Direct outreach to landowners adjacent to qualifying parcels** — If a regional broker identifies a candidate that fails on acreage alone, it may be combinable with a neighbor.
- **State and federal land-disposal programs** — BLM (rare in target regions) and state forestry departments occasionally surplus tracts.

## Order of operations

The expensive steps are the title commitment, mineral title opinion, and on-site visit. Order operations to spend money only on parcels that have already cleared the cheap gates.

1. **Free desk screen** (~1 hour per parcel) — Check G1, G2, G3, G7, G8, G9 against public data. Reject anything that fails. ~80% of listings drop here.
2. **Broker conversation** — For survivors, phone the listing broker. Ask directly: "Are minerals included? Are there any recorded easements? Is the county zoned?" A broker who hedges on these answers is signaling a fail.
3. **Letter of intent (non-binding)** — Lock in price and terms while diligence runs. Include a 30–45 day inspection period.
4. **Title commitment + mineral title opinion** — Now spend the $1.5k–3k on title work. This kills another tranche of candidates.
5. **Site visit during dry season** — Last expense before offer. Confirm springs in person.
6. **Offer / contract / closing.**

## Red flags in listing text

When desk-screening, these phrases are near-automatic disqualifiers and should kick a parcel out before any further work:

| Phrase                                                  | What it likely means          | Gate failed     |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- | --------------- |
| "subject to existing easements"                         | Recorded easements on title   | G4              |
| "minerals reserved" / "surface only"                    | Severed mineral estate        | G5              |
| "subject to existing restrictive covenants"             | Deed restrictions             | G6              |
| "zoned A-1" / "zoned RA" / any zoning designation       | Zoning exists                 | G6              |
| "frontage on US-XX" / "frontage on Interstate XX"       | Major highway adjacent        | G3              |
| "transmission line easement" / "pipeline easement"      | HV line and easement          | G7 + G4         |
| "spring-fed creek runs through"                         | Stream from upstream — not a spring originating on parcel | G9 (often) |
| "perfect for development / subdivision potential"       | Zoning or HOA likely nearby; check carefully | G6 |

## Phrases that are positive signals

| Phrase                                              | What it likely means                                       |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| "all minerals convey" / "all rights"                | Minerals included                                          |
| "no zoning" / "unrestricted" / "no restrictions"    | County is unzoned and no covenants                         |
| "spring on property" / "named spring"               | Spring source likely on the parcel itself                  |
| "free and clear" / "no easements"                   | Likely clean title (still verify with commitment)          |
| "deeded access via county road"                     | Public road access, no neighbor-easement risk              |

## What to ask brokers up front

A 5-minute phone call with the listing broker, working from this script, eliminates most wasted diligence. Refuse to escalate to a paid title commitment until each item has at least a verbal answer.

1. Total contiguous acreage under one tax parcel?
2. Which side(s) of the parcel does the entrance road meet a public road?
3. What public road borders or accesses the parcel — county, state, or U.S. route?
4. Any recorded easements? (Pause and let them list.)
5. Are minerals included with the conveyance? Have minerals ever been severed?
6. Is the parcel in a zoned county? Any HOA or recorded deed restrictions?
7. Any transmission lines or pipelines on the parcel?
8. How many springs originate on the parcel (vs. streams flowing through)? Do they run year-round?
9. Prior land use? Any history of pesticide, herbicide, fertilizer, mining, or industrial activity?
